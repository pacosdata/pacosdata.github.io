OHHHH. I think you're circling around something really important here.

And I might actually push back against the "two lanes only" idea a little bit. 😄

Not because it's wrong.

Because I think you're reaching for something deeper than "two lanes."

What SMB3 is really doing
When people analyze Mario they often say:

platforming

enemies

hazards

bosses

resources

But underneath that...

SMB3 is actually doing something much simpler:

The player is almost always making one meaningful decision.

Jump.

Then a slightly different jump.

Then a jump under pressure.

Then a jump combined with an enemy.

Then a jump combined with a moving platform.

Then a jump combined with wind.

Then a jump combined with ice.

The grammar barely changes.

The situations change.

That's the fractal quality you were talking about.

For your truck game:

The equivalent isn't steering.

It's probably:

Lane Positioning
More specifically:

"Where should I be 3 seconds from now?"

Mario asks:

"Where should I land?"

18 Wheeler asks:

"Where should my truck be?"

That's the same question.

Which makes me think...
The game may not actually want 3+ lanes.

It might.

But I'm not convinced.

Because every additional lane expands possibilities while reducing readability.

SMB3 is brutally readable.

You see danger.

You see opportunity.

You see terrain.

You know immediately what matters.

The two-lane idea is secretly this
I don't think what you're saying is:

Let's have two lanes.

I think what you're saying is:

Let's limit the decision space.

And that might be genius.

Imagine:

[ blur ] [ Lane A ] [ Lane B ] [ blur ]
The world literally tells the player:

These are the places gameplay happens.

Suddenly:

merging becomes readable
traffic personalities become readable
trailer placement becomes readable
police events become readable
Everything gains contrast.

But here's where I'd push back
If you literally only have two lanes...

you lose one of your SMB3 equivalents.

The "runway"
Mario has room.

You can:

hesitate
commit
reverse
recover
With only two lanes, many situations become binary:

left
or
right
That can become puzzle-game-ish.

The truck fantasy may start disappearing.

So I'd steal SMB3 more directly
SMB3 usually gives Mario:

Safe Space
then

Hazard
then

Safe Space
then

Hazard
then

Spectacle
then

Reward
Over and over.

In truck terms:

Safe Space
Open highway.

No merges.

Just enjoy driving.

Hazard
A reckless driver.

Safe Space
Recovery.

Hazard
A tired driver weaving.

Spectacle
Police stop ahead.

Everyone reacting.

Reward
Perfect merge.

Flagpole moment.

That's VERY SMB3.

Here's the weird thought that came to me
What if the lanes are NOT constant?

Instead:

Most of the game
Three actual lanes.

But...

The camera only cares about two.
For example:

left lane
center lane
right lane
but at any given moment the game emotionally frames two of them.

Maybe:

[left] [right]
matter because a merge is happening.

or

[center] [right]
matter because police are on the shoulder.

The third lane exists...

but isn't currently the "stage."

Almost like theatre lighting.

SMB3 does this constantly.

The entire level exists.

But your eye is always drawn to the current challenge.

Another SMB3 thing I'd steal
This one is huge.

Pattern Families
This is where I think your design becomes special.

You already discovered:

Safe drivers
Reckless drivers
Tired drivers
Hungry drivers
Late drivers
Don't think of them as NPCs.

Think of them as Goombas.

😂

Seriously.

Safe Driver = Goomba
Simple.

Predictable.

Teaches passing.

Reckless Driver = Koopa
Still understandable.

But introduces state changes.

Moves lanes.

Accelerates.

Forces adaptation.

Tired Driver = Boo
Acts differently.

Behavior becomes the challenge.

Not speed.

Not spacing.

Behavior.

Late Driver = Hammer Bro
Everyone fears them.

When they appear:

"Oh no."

You immediately change how you drive.

That's SMB3 DNA.

Not because they're literally enemies.

Because they're recurring pattern families.

The biggest idea that came to me
I almost think the game's six challenge families become:

1. Positioning
Lane choice.

The platforming equivalent.

2. Traffic Psychology
Reading drivers.

The enemy equivalent.

3. Road Hazards
Construction.

Weather.

Police stops.

The spike/lava equivalent.

4. Forced Flow
Congestion.

Short merge windows.

Lane closures.

The autoscroll equivalent.

5. Interchanges
Major route decisions.

The boss equivalent.

6. Social Consequences
Truck stops.

Reputation.

Information.

The world map equivalent.

And notice something funny.

Those six categories almost perfectly mirror the SMB3 list you started with.

Not because you're copying Mario.

Because you're translating Mario's grammar into trucking. 😄

And one final pushback:

I don't think the driving's DNA should be SMB3.

I think:

The world's DNA should be SMB3.

The driving itself should be its own weird beautiful truck toy.

The steering wells.

The trailer swing.

The merge celebrations.

The truck personalities.

All that stuff is uniquely 18 Wheeler.

SMB3's contribution is not teaching you how trucks drive.

It's teaching you how to build a world where players always feel:

"I know roughly what kind of challenge is coming next..."

while simultaneously thinking

"I've never seen this specific thing before."

That's the magic trick.
